I. Overview

Field noteWhere Dorn's sons build walls, the Mountain Breakers remove the ground those walls stand on.

The Mountain Breakers are an Imperial Fists successor chapter of siege engineers who win wars by reshaping the ground they fight on. They are legendary for terrain manipulation at planetary scale: flattened mountain ranges, broken ocean bedrock.

The chapter is mostly engineers and heavy machinery. They think in geological time, and that is both their greatest strength and their defining flaw.

II. Founding & Creed

The chapter was founded in the Second Founding, shortly after the Horus Heresy, when the Legions were broken into chapters.

Its founders were Imperial Fists who had built the defences of Terra and the Sol System under Rogal Dorn and fought in the Siege of Terra. They saw the traitors break through those walls anyway.

The founding lesson

Walls built on a world can fall. A world whose geology is the wall is far harder to break.

The creed

Every Imperial system should become a fortress the way Sol was: a network no enemy can penetrate. The chapter preaches it openly and proudly.

Admirers call it vision; critics find it alarming. It is why the chapter thinks in millennia. It is preparing for the next ten wars, not this one.

Dorn built walls on the world;
we make the world the wall.

Chapter motto · inscription

III. Early History & Homeworld

The chapter's first act was to reshape its future homeworld through a Long Fallow. Read the core sample from the surface down: newest layer on top, bedrock below.

  1. Surface · present

    The homeworld

    The first world the chapter reshaped, and the proof that the method works. Some regions are still settling, so the fortress monastery is constantly maintained and expanded rather than simply garrisoned. Its name is unrecorded.

  2. Layer · the move out

    The world is ready

    When the homeworld was ready, the chapter moved out. The forge lost its guests but kept the treaty.

  3. Layer · centuries of settling

    Guests of the forge

    While the homeworld settled for centuries, the young chapter was hosted by an allied forge world. The forge alliance was born here: shared recruitment began out of necessity, and the chapter grew up alongside the Skitarii.

    The forge watched the homeworld settle and learned how rich a reshaped world becomes. That is the root of its appetite for other Fallowed worlds.

  4. Layer · first Break

    The first Long Fallow

    The chapter's first act: breaking its future homeworld to a planned design.

  5. Layer · Second Founding

    The Legions broken

    Shortly after the Horus Heresy, the Legions were broken into chapters, and the Mountain Breakers were founded from the Imperial Fists.

  6. Bedrock

    The Siege of Terra

    The founders built the defences of Terra and Sol under Rogal Dorn, and watched the traitors break through them anyway.

IV. Doctrine & Way of War

The chapter prefers to secure and reshape a position rather than storm it. It is eager to work alongside other chapters and Imperial forces, taking on terraforming, fortification and holding ground while others attack.

Flooding

Drowning enemy positions by redirecting water, breaching reservoirs or opening the seabed.

Destabilizing

Undermining the ground beneath fortifications until it collapses.

Strategic bombardment

Breaking terrain at scale from above.

V. The Long Fallow

In extreme cases the chapter reshapes an entire world into a fortress. It does not end all life there, as Exterminatus does: it moves mountains, floods basins and raises walls of rock, and accepts the losses as the cost of building.

In the chapter's eyes it is building, never destroying. A Fallowed world keeps its place on the Imperial rolls, and people survive in the regions left untouched.

A world is a castle first
and a home second.

Chapter saying

What is a world for?

The real dispute is over what a world is for.

The chapter sees

Fortresses with garrisons.

vs

Governors, Ecclesiarchy & officials see

Populations, tithes and homes.

Neither side is wrong by its own logic, and the people under the new mountains get no say.

Story hooks

  • A city stands where the design calls for a new mountain range.
  • A planetary governor petitions the High Lords to stop a Fallow of their world.
  • A sergeant begins to doubt that the losses are worth the walls.

VII. Librarius

MainstreamOpenly respected

Geokinesis

Most librarians follow it: shaping rock, raising walls, causing tremors. It continues the Imperial Fists tradition and pushes it from fortification to breaking the earth.

RareControversial

Technomancy

A few librarians work directly on machines through psychic power. To the Mechanicus, a psyker reaching into a machine spirit is close to blasphemy. The forge tolerates them because they are useful, and watches them closely.

FrictionTechmarines serve the Machine God through ritual. Technomancers bypass the ritual entirely. The two groups do not get along.

VIII. Forge Alliance & Recruitment

The allied forge world (name unrecorded) hosted the young chapter, and the two have shared their recruits ever since.

Almost-brothers

Many Skitarii stood in the same trials as the Marines. The alliance is personal, not just a treaty.

Shared arsenal

Both forces are supplied by the same forge, so the chapter fields Skitarii weaponry alongside standard Astartes wargear.

IX. Tensions

Fault lines run through every relationship the chapter keeps.

1

The Inquisition

The mass casualties of a Fallow draw scrutiny, however firmly the chapter insists it builds rather than destroys.

2 Main conflict

The Mechanicus

A Fallowed world rich in exposed minerals looks like a future mining or forge world. The forge may arrive early, before the chapter considers the world ready.

3

Techmarines

Trained on Mars and sworn to the Machine God. An engineering chapter has far more of them than usual, giving the Mechanicus unusual influence inside it. When chapter and forge disagree, they are caught in the middle.

Story hook: a Techmarine who quietly passes the chapter's Fallow timelines to the forge.

4

Technomancers vs Techmarines

Ritual service to machine spirits against psychic shortcuts that bypass it.

5

Governors, Ecclesiarchy & Imperial officials

The “castle vs home” dispute over what a world is for.

X. Strengths & Flaws

Load-bearing

  • Massive campaigns and large set-piece battles.
  • Fortifying the positions of allies and Imperial forces, which is how they are most often deployed.
  • Holding and reshaping ground on a scale few others can match.

Fault lines

  • Neither fast nor eager to engage every enemy, and poor at swift attacks.
  • Their answer to a hard problem can be to leave and return in a thousand years, and that patience fails against foes who move quickly.
  • Strategies that rely on natural processes break down against enemies who can bend the natural order.
